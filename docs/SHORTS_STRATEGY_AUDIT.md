Format taught: roughly 20–30 second vertical mini-documentaries. Immediate curiosity/question, explanation over changing footage, then a short payoff. Visual segments around 2–4 seconds are suggestions from the tutorial, not YouTube requirements.

### What is useful for Mecha
Compress ONE genuine question into ONE satisfying explanation. Start with the visual puzzle, remove long greetings, connect each shot to the narration and deliver the answer before the ending.
Claude/ChatGPT can take complementary drafting/review roles; neither brand is required for a particular stage. vidIQ helps compare public examples. The voice, captions and shot list should be checked against actual duration, not assumed correct because an AI generated “30 seconds.”
This is a good fit for Tiene su Física: creator expertise, simple original diagrams and own voice create a distinct contribution. The selected satellite topic can become a first 25–40 second Short rather than a 6–8 minute pilot.

### What not to adopt
The tutorial explicitly advocates mixed synthetic/real footage so viewers do not notice AI. Mecha instead makes the actual required disclosure and never presents synthetic footage as real evidence.
TikTok download capability is not reuse permission. Generated animal behavior is not evidence that the behavior occurs. Fact-check any scientific premise taken from a competitor summary.
The shown editing remains manual: selecting clips, importing URLs, trimming, audio muting, formatting and checking captions. “99% automated” and “5–10 minutes per finished Short” are promotional assertions, not measured Mecha capability.

Example names reported by visual analysis: Trash Tier (@TrashTierLol), RankZilla (@RankZilla23), The Ranking Guy (@therankingguy_03). These are reference leads, not channels independently verified in this audit.
Format taught: a clear category/title, roughly 5–7 ranked clips, short labels, nonsequential playback and #1 last. A two-line colored heading and clip-count range are the creator's preferences, not algorithm or platform requirements.

### What is useful for Mecha
A ranking promises a clear endpoint, gives each segment a role and creates anticipation. Show the comparison criterion and why each item deserves its position. Ranking can be a narrative device for original commentary rather than a container for borrowed videos.
Proposed second track: technology/invention or everyday-engineering comparisons, theme/name subject to creator choice. Use original demonstrations, licensed material and explanatory judgments; do not automatically launch a generic fail channel.
Start by testing 3–5 explained items within 35–60 seconds. This is a Mecha hypothesis; do not misattribute it to the tutorial's 5–7 recommendation.

### What not to adopt
Changing clip order, adding numbers or a colored headline does not establish original educational value or copyright permission. Narration/commentary also does not automatically clear copyright.
Do not rank staged scenes as authentic achievements or make unsupported “best/most dangerous” claims. Define what is compared, date-sensitive quantities and limitations.
“Post every day or you cannot go viral” is not established causality. Choose a sustainable schedule based on rights, quality, production time and observed audience response.

## Tools, costs and alternatives
Official Viblo pricing page checked 2026-10-17: Starter $25/month (30 Shorts, 200 credits, fast mode up to 60s); Creator $49/month (90 Shorts, 500 credits, additional modes); Business $99/month (200 Shorts, 1,200 credits). Published annual prices are separate commitments. “Unlimited exports” does not mean unlimited generation.
The tutorials advertise a seven-day trial; current pricing retrieval did not substantiate its current availability. Check checkout, renewal and commercial rights before purchase. Pricing prominently lists commercial rights with Business, while marketing FAQ makes broader ownership claims; verify lower-tier voice/asset/output rights rather than assume.
No Viblo subscription, trial signup, external integration or AI voice replacement is approved by this audit.
- Existing route: Python + FFmpeg, original assets, optional Manim, own narration.
- Viblo route: faster ready-made browser editor, recurring spend/manual UI workflow; no supported API/MCP integration established.
- vidIQ: research layer, not the application's local renderer.
- ChatGPT/Claude: assist analysis/drafts; existing chat subscriptions do not fund application APIs.
- Manim: use only where a diagram teaches the concept; not a mandatory visual style for all Shorts.

## Proposed production flow
Creator chooses topic/track → public-example research → source/rights dossier → ORIGINAL brief and spoken script → timed shot plan → own recording → approved original/licensed assets → vertical edit and readable captions → factual/rights/disclosure review → private upload and creator release approval → metrics and cost review.
For ranking, also persist candidate order, editorial rank, playback order and ranking criterion. These are separate data concepts.
Keep a clip rights record: source/owner, acquisition method, permission/license evidence, permitted modifications/platform/commercial use, attribution and expiry where relevant. A social URL alone is insufficient.
An idea table should record reference evidence, originality angle, source confidence, asset feasibility, effort and learning goal; no invented probability of “going viral.”

## Application gap and next implementation slice
The open PR's local application currently renders LANDSCAPE 854×480 video, with card/orbit scenes, scene-level SRT, own-audio import, revisions and bundle handoff. It does NOT already implement vertical Shorts, real clip compositing, animated word captions, automatic speech alignment, ranking layouts or YouTube publishing.
Next implementation proposal, after format confirmation:
1. Explicit render profiles: Shorts 9:16 (1080×1920 target; smaller vertical draft) with configurable safe regions for mobile UI; preserve landscape as an optional profile.
2. Original/licensed asset-import model with rights metadata, crop/fit and audio muting; no unauthorized downloader.
3. Timed text/legible captions and voice alignment; support own recordings first.
4. Two configurable formats: micro-explainer and ranking/commentary, with meaningful content variation.
5. Revision-scoped review of script, footage rights and disclosure. Existing companion reviews remain manual until implemented.
6. Three pilots per track, compare effort/quality and available engaged views, viewed-versus-swiped, watch duration/percentage and feedback. No universal target/causal claim from a small sample.
7. Test 9:16 dimensions, crop safety, label placement, audio duration and revision/cache identity; verify actual rendered videos before release.

This audit changes strategy/documentation only; it does not implement the new renderer, commit copyrighted clips, change existing account settings or publish videos. Keep PR #3 open for review.
